Astronomers detected an unexplained high-energy radio pulse radiating from a distant magnetar across the Perseus cluster.
Initial astrophysical models dismissed the signal as routine orbital sensor noise or satellite telemetry interference.
Deep spectroscopic calibration revealed sharp harmonic intervals, definitively ruling out thermal background radiation.
Synchronized observatory logs across four continents confirmed an identical millisecond magnetic pulsation pattern.
The recorded anomaly established an empirical baseline, transforming modern theoretical models of compact stellar collapse.
